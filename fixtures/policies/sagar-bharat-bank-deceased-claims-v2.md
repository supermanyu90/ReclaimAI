# Sagar Bharat Bank: settlement of claims of deceased depositors

FICTIONAL POLICY FOR A SYNTHETIC DEMO. Sagar Bharat Bank does not exist. Nothing here describes any real bank's procedure.

## §1 Scope
This policy covers savings accounts and fixed deposits held in India by a deceased depositor. It does not cover accounts held outside India, lockers, or loans.

## §2 Registered nominee
Where a nomination is registered, the bank settles the claim in favour of the nominee on receipt of: (a) the claim form, (b) a copy of the death certificate, (c) the nominee's identity and address proof (KYC), and (d) the passbook, statement or deposit receipt. Payment to the nominee discharges the bank; it does not decide who inherits the money. The nominee holds it on behalf of the legal heirs.

## §3 Survivorship mandate
For joint accounts with an "either or survivor" or "former or survivor" mandate, the surviving holder may continue or close the account on producing (a) a copy of the death certificate and (b) the survivor's KYC. No nominee or legal-heir documents are needed.

## §4 No nominee and no survivor
Where there is neither a nomination nor a survivorship mandate, the bank settles in favour of the legal heirs. For an undisputed claim where the total balance is up to Rs. 15,00,000, the bank accepts (a) the claim form signed by all legal heirs, (b) a copy of the death certificate, (c) a letter of indemnity, (d) an affidavit of legal heirship, and (e) KYC of each legal heir. A succession certificate is not required in this case. Above Rs. 15,00,000, the bank requires a succession certificate, letter of administration or probate.

## §5 Disputed claims
Where the legal heirs dispute the claim, the bank does not settle until it receives a court order, succession certificate, letter of administration or probate.

## §6 Timelines
The bank aims to settle a claim within 15 days of receiving complete documents. This is a target, not a guarantee.
